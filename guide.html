Your first open mic, demystified

← Back to the mic list source

What an open mic actually is

A room, a sign-up list, and a line of people doing 3–5 minutes each. Most of the audience is other comics waiting their turn. Nobody is discovered at an open mic and nobody's career ends at one — it's a gym, not a show. Everyone in that room did a first mic once, and everyone remembers theirs. The bar for your first time is: show up, get through your minutes, come back.

The four sign-up systems

  • Online list — you book a slot ahead (TicketTailor, Tixr, a Google form, an Instagram post). Least stressful for a first-timer: you know you're up before you commute.
  • In-person list — a paper or iPad list opens 15–30 minutes before the mic. Arrive early; popular lists fill in minutes.
  • Phone — a few old-school rooms take sign-ups by phone. Call in the afternoon.
  • Lottery / bucket — names drawn from a bucket. You might not get up. Fine once you're doing mics weekly; frustrating for a first attempt — start with a guaranteed list instead.

What it costs

Typically $5, sometimes free. Some rooms have a one- or two-item minimum instead of (or on top of) the fee. Budget $10 and you're covered almost anywhere. You're paying for stage time, not admission.

The light, and the one rule that matters

Near the end of your time someone flashes a light (a phone, usually) from the back. That means wrap up now — finish the sentence, say thanks, get off. Running past the light is the single fastest way to be remembered badly by every comic and host in the room. Respect it and you're already ahead of half the list.

Etiquette that makes people like you

  • Stay and watch. Doing your set and immediately leaving is noticed. Watch at least the few comics before and after you.
  • Be an audience. Laugh when things are funny. You want the same when you're up.
  • Don't talk during sets. Even from the back. Especially from the back.
  • Notes: most rooms don't care if you glance at notes; a few ban them — it's listed per mic on this site. Either way, knowing your set beats reading it.

Preparing your first three minutes

  1. Write more than you need, then cut to the 3 minutes you can say naturally.
  2. Say it out loud, standing up, at least ten times. Out loud is different from in your head.
  3. Record yourself once on your phone. You'll cut two more sentences after hearing it.
  4. Plan your first and last lines exactly. The middle can flex; the ends shouldn't.
Bring your set as a voice memo and listen on the train there. Record your actual set from your pocket, too — the recording is where the real learning happens, usually on the ride home.

"Bringer" shows — know the word

A bringer is a show where you only perform if you bring paying audience members. That's a different economy from an open mic. Every mic listed on this site is a true open mic — no bringing required. If a "new talent night" somewhere asks you to bring five friends, that's a bringer; go in with open eyes.

Your first night, step by step

  1. Pick an online-signup mic from the list (filter: Online signup). Book a slot.
  2. Arrive 20 minutes early. Tell the host it's your first mic — hosts are almost universally kind to first-timers.
  3. Order your drink if there's a minimum. Sit near the front half; laughs carry better there.
  4. Do your three minutes. Watch the light. Say thanks.
  5. Stay for a few more comics. Then go home and listen to your recording.
  6. Book the next one that night, while you still have the adrenaline. The second mic is the one that makes you a comic.
